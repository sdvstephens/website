# Trump and Freud

## Brief Psychology of Fascism


In *Massenpsychologie und Ich-Analyse*, Sigmund Freud outlined the first major psychoanalytic analysis of the modern political mass. Though Theodor Adorno insisted the problem lay not mainly in the realm of psychology, acknowledging its subservient nature to that of the base—economics—he highlighted, nonetheless, the importance of said psychological analysis in terms of the authoritarian personality lending itself to fascism. Freud had a bit of Marxian clairvoyance on this matter, seeing the distant tidal wave of fascism approaching far before it rose to prominence: he conjectured that the *Massenseele* (the group mind) had its own political motives, separate from that of the logical, rational, individual: affect, symbols, myth; these bonds that brought people together—brought them as fasces bound with an iron band—are erotic libidinal drives; that is to say, beyond the sexual, these libidinal group solidarity bonds were broadly erotic in nature. The resulting behavior is not, despite all appearances to look as such, something trans-historical. That is to say, fascism is not a lapse of regression. It is, instead, a simulation of that regression. It is modern and artificial. The simulation allows people enraptured by a fascistic wave to fantasize about their release from society and morality, reducing instead to instinct; fully embracing their full human power without apology, breaking, rupturing through morality (perhaps you more heartedly agree with the characterization, now, of this erotic nature). Nietzsche, worshiped by many a fascist, himself positioned as arguably a fellow-traveller, wished to bring about an embrace of the raw self—your archaic primitive form, the inner animal; the path to the *übermensch* then is penetrating through morality (much like our aforementioned fascistic rupturing) into the primitive reversion (*dies bedarf hier keiner weiteren Erörterung, da eine solche den Rahmen der vorliegenden Abhandlung überschritte*). The absurd and childish nature of the *Massenseele* needn’t be lost on me: Adorno, referencing the work of Gustave le Bon and *Psychologie des Foules* notes, in his work Freudian Theory and the Pattern of Fascist Propaganda, that the mass political societal entity was an irrational entity; “it may well be the secret of fascist propaganda that it simply takes men for what they are: the true children of today’s standardized mass culture largely robbed of autonomy and spontaneity, instead of setting goals the realization of which would transcend the psychological status quo no less than the social one.” 

Indeed, robbed of their autonomy and spontaneity, the *Massenseele* seek a great leader, one who has embraced their inner man-beast; upon the advent of a leader, the group mind idealize and identify with the leader—they exist as the common man and the *übermensch*; they are just like you, and they are nothing like you. This contradiction is summed up well by Adorno:
> “Even the fascist leader’s startling symptoms of inferiority, his resemblance to ham actors and asocial psychopaths, is thus anticipated in Freud’s theory. For the sake of those parts of the follower’s narcissistic libido which have not been thrown into the leader image but remain attached to the follower’s own ego, the [*übermensch*] must still resemble the follower and appear as his “enlargement.” Accordingly, one of the basic devices of personalized fascist propaganda is the concept of the “great little man,” a person who suggests both omnipotence and the idea that he is just one of the folks, a plain, red-blooded American, untainted by material or spiritual wealth. Psychological ambivalence helps to work a social miracle. The leader gratifies the follower’s twofold wish to submit to authority and to be the authority himself.”

The eroticism, then, is not just from the binding of the fasces, but, too, from simultaneously dominating and being dominated. It is desirable; thus, performance, play, itself is thrust into view; the aesthetics of order and loss of control: long banners and clean Neo-classical architecture unite with brash and impassioned speeches, painting an aesthetic reminiscent of culinary theatre.

This strictly reactionary performance, this display of art, is the fascism of the future; cult ritual performances (Benjamin’s epitome of *l’art pour l’art: fiat ars— pereat mundus*) wherein leaders “lose control” and show “lack of restraint” as mere falsehoods, refusing to actually threaten the very liberal democratic structure which bore it. The deceptive nature of the performances does not detract from their efficacy as long as the *Massenseele* feel as if they are releasing their inner man-beast. This digression is not strictly relevant to the material at hand, so I will end it here; however, of final note on this point, the fascists attempt to supersume bourgeois society through autocracy, but find themselves incapable of doing so long-term: the base is far more loyal to the bourgeois than to the fascist. 

In the developmental schema of Freud, the young child begins with an oral fixation: gratification, incorporation, and validation of the mouth and its oral pleasantries. Speech is the mode then of simultaneous consumption and the act of being consumed; the lies, the childish diatribes are not to be taken trivially: it is the substance of freedom for the masses. The character of the fascistic leader follows this prepubertal classification of orality—focused on pleasure via speaking and rambling incessantly, hoping to befool others. It becomes unbound by rationality and reason: it is mythic repetition.  

We now turn to the modern day; Freud and Adorno’s analysis of fascist psychological methodology, in almost its whole, remains true, sans a minor oversight of what will be termed **boring fascism**. 

## Donald Trump and the MAGA base

Donald Trump, archetypal of the oral character, of course, utilizing, consciously or not, every tool of the typology with MAGA, conceives of reversion and returning directed to the masses. The mythic repetition of "Make America Great Again" finds its importance and its continuance in the ritual action itself. Indeed, again decreeing a reversion to wholeness which never existed: the (political) base’s primal object of desire.

There is something evangelical here; it is the duty then of members of this base to seek converts en masse, yet without regard to approach, thus becoming one which robs autonomy of the members of the evangelizing. And who are they to evangelize for other than the salvific and deific figure of Trump? Primed by decades of submission to pastoral authority and anti-intellectualism—familiar with the erotics of domination and surrender—requires no new psychological molding to accommodate the fascist leader. The identification with the leader as ego-ideal, the hostility toward out-groups as the negative bond that reinforces internal cohesion, the replacement of individual critical faculty with the affective logic of the mass—all present, all functioning as theorized. The follower's narcissistic libido is split precisely as Adorno described: partially invested in the leader-image, partially retained in the ego, requiring that the leader remain recognizable as an enlargement of the self rather than an alien superior. Trump's vast vulgarity, his fast food and gaudy gold-plated aesthetics, his transparent mendacity pose not obstacles to identification but in it, its conditions for identification in its entirety. He must be one of them. He must also be more than them. It is, however, here we note a digression from Freud’s original analysis; the material (substance) of the matter and its philosophies are just as important as the resulting form it consequently assumes; that is to say, the morality, lack thereof, aesthetics, and lack thereof are essential to the resulting form the Freudian psychoanalysis gives. The analytic theory, however, finds its basis in bourgeois culture, and thus it is these philosophies that must underpin the fascistic analysis: with Trump, this does not hold. 

Despite barbarism, the fascism of Adorno’s time englamoured itself with a certain cultural density: it recruited philosophers, architectural and aesthetic doctrine, cinematography, and the entire apparatus of *Gesamtkunstwerk*. The fascism of the early twentieth century emerged from bourgeois culture and carried, within it, the marks of bourgeois *Bildung* (formation). Its leaders, regardless of psychological pathologies, underwent *Bildung*: the long, intergenerational epigenetic process of cultural, intellectual, and aesthetic formation through which the bourgeois subject is constituted. Within this *Bildung* are the aesthetic values which, as Marcuse argued in *Eros and Civilization*, do not merely decorate the psyche but participate in the construction of the superego and ego-ideal themselves. That is to say, the *Bildung* constitutes the concept and identification of the self. 

Trump underwent no such *Bildung*. Here, observations of Slavoj Žižek's become indispensable: Trump exists not as a member of the bourgeois cultural order but as its perpetual aspirant, orbiting the class whose cultural authority he covets but whose formative processes he has never undergone. He is, in this regard, structurally homeomorphic to Thatcher at Balmoral as depicted in *The Crown*: present among the aristocracy, tolerated, never absorbed; she buys her own furniture (and packs her own clothes) and in so doing reveals the irreducibility of the gap. But Thatcher, at minimum, was the ideological coherence of neoliberalism incarnate; she was the project. Trump is not even that. He is the periphery without a center, aspiration without formation, the desire for bourgeois legitimacy without any of the cultural substance that would make such legitimacy possible. The golden gaudy garish form of Trump’s psychopathological architecture is an LLM-reproduction, a bastardized stochastic parrot of the bourgeois formation. 

Trump and his base, seem then, to follow quite well the analysis of Freud and Adorno, however, there is of note an imperfection: that is to say, due to the tripartite analysis of Freud relying on the bourgeois *Bildung* as essential to the formation of the psyche, it overlooks the fascist which wishes to be the liberal; the fascist who goes to operas and musicals thinking it enlightened but prefers *Phantom of the Opera* to Offenbach’s *Les Contes d’Hoffman*. Trump, unable to be an accepted member of the bourgeois cultural superstructure, chooses not to attempt to destroy it but to imitate from his twisted conception of what the cultural sphere actually is through whatever means possible. Wherein is it, then, that this tripartic analysis fails? It is in the identification of the crude simulacra of bourgeois culture, clearly and boldly marked by the advent of boring fascism. The intellectualization of fascism that pervaded during early conception is notably absent from the modern MAGA movement; the internal secrecy of conspiracy is instead on display for all to view in its absurdity and ugliness. The material and visual culture of MAGA here is now brought to our attention: mass-produced borderline scam/ sham merchandise, Trump steaks, Trump vodka, Trump phones, all with his mockery of gold plastered at every opportunity—these designs through whatever interpretation that may be given do not act as agents of the avant-garde but instead as the “sloppification” of aesthetics due to indifference or even disillusionment. 

Then we might conjecture: it is not only morality that this modern fascistic movement attempts to break through and shed, but it is, too, aesthetics, which the movement seeks to make itself free from. Yes, indeed, this boring fascism after the evacuation of *Bildung* from its leadership creates a gilded *mise en scène* attempting to fool its audience and entrap them in the aesthetic wave of shedding aesthetics. Indeed, too, emblematic of the paradoxically presented shedding and acceptance of aesthetics by the fascistic regime, military parades and constant America-first propaganda become the very accepted aesthetics of this ugly, raw fascism.  The Nietzschean penetration through civilizational prohibition into archaic power has extended itself to the realm of aesthetics: putting on a circus show, performing strong-man aesthetics of violating aesthetics and morality. This is boring fascism. 

One might object to this classification of boring fascism, noting the historic nature of Italian Futurists who aligned themselves with the fascism of Mussolini despite their aesthetic avant-garde innovations, thus allowing us to reasonably deduce that the fascistic and the avant-garde are not here oppositional, and thus the MAGA base can be classified as the avant-garde, for their boldness of aesthetic violations. To hold a mentality would be akin to intellectual suicide; causality here is inverted: in the case of the fascists of old, we move from aesthetic transgression towards political deployment, but in modernity, we start at the fascistic and decide our aesthetics from there. But more, then the entire apparatus under discussion—moral simulation, aesthetic transgression, leader-identification, the *Massenseele* itself—belongs to the superstructure. It is a manipulation of superstructure, not the base, and superstructural changes are under no obligation to produce consistent or logical formations. The base by no means requires coherence from its ideological emanations; only that they function. To take a brief example from the modern day: superstructural indifference to logical consistency accounts for the otherwise inexplicable formations of gay furry Nazis, Latinos for Trump, and LGBTQ for Trump, among others. 

Assuming, forgoing analysis accuracy, i.e., that Trumpist fascism is not fascism-done-poorly but a structurally distinct modality produced by the absence of *Bildung* in its leadership and the superstructural chaos of the medium in its entirety, then the approach commonly employed as a reaction to the Trump base is misguided. The aesthetic poverty of Trump’s golden kitsch movement and its mockery instead build up and reinforce the ideological grounding of the (political) base; thus, mockery sustains it, enflames it (this is by no means an assertion that without such there would be no aforementioned movement, merely this is an analysis of the outcomes resulting from the superstructure, a truly sufficient condition, as with most Marxist, Freudian, or critical theory analyses is always the base). 
The hope that boring fascism’s aesthetic impoverishment makes it somehow less dangerous than its historical predecessors—that its lack of Riefenstahl, its absence of Speer, its substitution of QAnon for Schmitt, renders it a diminished thing—can be the most dangerous error of all. The base, as Adorno reminded us at the outset, does not require the superstructure to be elegant, beautiful, or intellectually coherent. It requires only that it function. And the libidinal mechanisms function. The identification functions. The simulated regression functions. Boring fascism is no less fascism for its boredom; it is fascism that has dispensed with the pretense that the superstructure need be anything more than what it minimally must be to bind the fasces together. The iron band holds, whether polished or rusted.
