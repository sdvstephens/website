# thoughts, musings

Sometimes I compile lists. I am not sure why I do this, but it becomes obsessive. I create lists of composers, critical theorists, books, languages, waters, people, names, buildings, types of fabric, parts of x-ray machines. In some sense, it is therapeutic. But these lists take up my time. They feel like living and so I substitute my life for these lists. Who do I want to read? What do I want to know? What do I want to create? Instead, one might make a list and have fantastically lived these lives. 

A friend told me I should write a blog the other day. I told them I would. I forgot. But, hey, I am writing it today! Better late than later. 

I think about all the ways to love a person. The different boundaries we draw between types of relationships and types of love to more easily categorize these uncaptureable feelings end up being so arbitrary we concern ourselves, more, by having a Platonic ideal and contorting love to form around it. Love is illogical; you do not love someone because their eyes, their face, their intelligence, you do not love for even the kindness and the friendliness of others, no---first you love and then form description around it. You contort it to your Platonic ideal, approaching, not quite reaching. That does not make the love any less beautiful, any less genuine, it simply means it is altered. This distortion's goodness or badness quality is not tethered to the aforementioned. 

Yesterday I was trying to figure out how to properly represent supremums and infimums using *Hasse diagrams* for things like posets. Hasse diagrams come up so often; I am unsure of their limitations. I once conjectured they may be useful in attempting to describe Hempel's definitions of science using non-classical logic. Maybe it's gobbledygook, maybe it's interesting. 

Something I have had to learn is that, despite novelty, there is so much math that fails at "reasonable interestingness," by which I mean, it fails to properly capture something interesting to the greater literature (or in context of it). The issue is I often find these things interesting. Usefulness is dubious at best in such realms of interest so far. I do like to think that perhaps something will come of these orbital musings. 

Speaking of lists of things, I recently compiled a list of movies I want to see with others. I have no idea what half of the movies are, but they were recommended by people and figures I respect, so I imagine, at the very least, it will give some insight into them. 

I think I am going to post one of the essays I wrote for college. I shall do that after.
