# The Penguin

My absorption of others and subsequent regurgitation as art has carved out a permanent, beggingly permeable, slot on the other side of the veil. I can sometimes taste them in my mouth---pale imitations. How could I have known so wholly, how could I have been entirely? When did I stop? The transition isn't quite clear, it's muddied with childhood dreams and love, warped and twisted, hopeful and wondrous. 

In 2007, Werner Herzog, known for his films that force our intense gaze into the absurd, released a documentary film shot in Antarctica. I'm sure you know where this is going by now. Near the end of the film, Herzog interviews David Ainley, a penguin scientist, and fatefully asks *is there a such thing as insanity in a penguin?* 

I found a PhD thesis on animal psychopathology related to insanity while scouring Wikipedia: Animal Madness by Laurel Braitman. 

I have for a long time been trying to categorize this veil. It has proven difficult. The nature of it eludes me and I find myself often questioning if understanding is even possible (*I know it exists, it *must**). My discoveries have not been fruitless, however; I have information, though it is very likely a great number of you already know of its illusory powers, it tells us of loves and lies and joys and pains and fears and hopes and dreams and shortcomings and successes and and and; does the veil amplify in its falsity as time marches on? 

Braitman's argues that we, the disordered, see proxy and reflection in these animals and their repetitive, obsessive, depressive behaviors. I wonder if during the Paleolithic Neanderthals, and what we triumphantly call *proto-humans*, looked into our eyes, saw our madness and wondered if we were truly ego dystonic, as they were. 

The lone penguin in Braitman's eyes simply does what the penguin does, in Werner's eyes he claims to see as is: nature; but in the penguin's eyes I see something far more. It is not a mirror. It is not falsity. It is not even me. 

**or maybe not.**
