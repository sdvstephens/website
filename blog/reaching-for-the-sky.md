# Reaching for The Sky


I am not quite so sure when it began—maybe when I was 7 or so—but I have the tendency to lie down on surfaces and tower an arm up lazily, balancing itself on the bones below, and grasp at sources of light and whatever it is that exists behind them. 

On Brain FM - Recuerdos De La Alhambra by Francisco Tárrega performed by Andrés Segovia. 

It allows me to feel closer to notions of the divine and, in my chest, feel the pressure of the transcendental lowering its weighty body upon me; a weighted blanket from a bright light. 

In one of the engineering buildings I found myself in an empty meeting room. I did the usual tasks, maths and tutoring, music and nonsense. A wave of exhaustion reached me. Perhaps due to my terrible sleep over the past couple of weeks or because the adderall wore off. At some point I got out of my seat and slowly walked to the center of the lengthy meeting table, trailing my hands along the edges as I got closer and closer towards the gravitational center of the table. I felt called. I lied down. I looked up. There was a bright light emanating from behind the ceiling: it was a painted white ceiling nestled and hidden behind the metal façade that was the initially believed ceiling. The island chandelier found itself reflecting upon that white background, lighting it up. The light behind the light. There really was something there after all: another ceiling. 

Handel’s Sarabande, with a bit of punctual irony, began playing from my phone, and I reached towards the light behind the light. And I cried. I began focusing on the object in front of me—my hand—instead of that great light behind. There was a cut on the back of my hand (I do not recall how this ended up here). My fingers had a slight tremor and my skin seemed as if it had been screaming for moisture and recently gave up its self-preservational protests. 

Now I am here. I realized I have written so little about those things I do. Those rituals. The great intensity of feelings that constantly inhabit my body, good, bad, joyous, grieving, and each feeling beyond: all intense. All overwhelming. 

I have ASD—Autism Spectrum Disorder—and this leads some people to believe I have deficits in feeling (by all means this myth certainly aided by the fact of my flat affect). I do not. I feel so much. So much all the time.
