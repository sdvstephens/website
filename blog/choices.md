# choices


It is often that I am paralyzed by choice. My friends jest with me, drawing similarities between my indecision and constant lactose-induced stomach aches and Chidi Anagonye's infamous stomach-ache-decision-paralysis that so constantly plagues him in the show *The Good Place* (excellent show, highly recommend it if you have not yet seen it). I digress. 

After concluding some biology studies, in the commons room on the second floor of Harvard's Smith Campus Center, I made my way to a deep recess in the wall, just past Harvard's *fancy* new professional head shot machine---free photography at your fingertips, which contained a vending machine. I knew I was hungry, but more so, I knew that if I left the commons I would be unable to return without the aid of others (card access issues), so vending machine it was. So many choices, so many flavors, so little money. Choice paralysis might come as a symptom of poverty at times, others a symptom of *obsession* (obsession with optimization of experience, of time); this case was a clear one---poverty stricken choice limbo: my money needed to stretch further than would allow for fanciful tasting. *Should I get the chips or the protein bar? What if I got the protein bar with the sparkling apple cider? No, no, that totals over $7, far too much for me to be flagrantly spending right now. Well what will it be, then? Chips or the bar. Hmm.* Indecision resulted in me choosing neither the chips nor the bar. 

I recall another time, when I was with my partner, exploring ice cream shops along the shopping and confectionary district Newbury St., and I could not for the love of me decide on J. P. Licks or Ben & Jerry's. J. P. Licks had a far more inviting atmosphere, raw brick, a seating area filled with earth tones and dark comforts, but I had been here before, thus was it *really* the optimal choice if I am ice cream curiosity? Ben and Jerry's has more of the flavor I might be looking for, a shorter line, gluten free treats for my partner, and a less crowded environment. Its interior was also painted white, which I detested. The overhead lights garishly illuminated the shop. How silly am I? Attempting to optimize ice cream choice. I am not even the one paying and yet I still and struck with walking back and forth to test out the environments of each establishment. What if I needed to try the seats? What if I didn't like their spoons? 

We ended up eating at Ben and Jerry's and it was satisfactory.

The choice, when made, is liberating. But the process by which we arrive at this choice is excruciating: the mental subterranean tussle and struggle, for me, breaks out from within my mind and rears its unsure self to surrounding spectators.
