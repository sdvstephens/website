We will let the real numbers themselves ($\RR$) be an axiom. (Is that to say that the class of the real numbers is $\RR \coloneqq \{x \mid x \in \QQ \text{ or } x \in \PP\}$, where $\PP$ is the irrational numbers?)

:::remark Correction
The real numbers are not themselves an axiom. Apostol takes $\RR$ as an undefined object and lists properties of it as axioms (the field, order and completeness axioms below). It is true that $\RR = \QQ \cup \PP$, but Apostol does not introduce $\RR$ that way: the irrationals are defined later, as the real numbers that are not rational.
:::

There are $3$ groups of axioms: the field axioms, the order axioms, and the completeness axiom.
