## Free groups

:::definition Free group
The **free group** $F_n$ on $n$ generators $a_1, \dots, a_n$ consists of the words
$$
a_{i_1}^{m_1} \cdots a_{i_k}^{m_k}, \qquad m_1, \dots, m_k \neq 0.
$$
It is the “largest” group generated by $n$ elements: all others are $\cong$ to quotients of $F_n$.
:::

If $G$ is generated by $g_1, \dots, g_n \in G$, we define a homomorphism $\varphi : F_n \surjto G$ by $a_i \mapsto g_i$, i.e.
$$
\prod a_{i_j}^{m_j} \mapsto \prod g_{i_j}^{m_j}.
$$

:::definition Finitely presented
$G$ is **finitely presented** if the kernel of $\varphi$ is the smallest normal subgroup of $F_n$ containing some finite subset $\{r_1, \dots, r_k\} \subseteq F_n$.
:::

:::example
$$
\ZZ^n \cong \langle a_1, \dots, a_n \mid a_i a_j a_i^{-1} a_j^{-1}\ \forall i, j \rangle, \qquad S_3 \cong \langle s_1, s_2 \mid s_1^2,\ s_2^2,\ (s_1 s_2)^3 \rangle.
$$
:::

In general
$$
G \cong \langle \underbrace{a_1, \dots, a_n}_{\text{generators}} \mid \underbrace{r_1, \dots, r_k}_{\text{relations}} \rangle, \qquad\text{so}\qquad G \cong F_n / \langle \text{conjugates of } r_1, \dots, r_k \rangle.
$$

$$
\begin{CD}
F_n = \langle a_1, \dots, a_n \rangle @>{\text{quotient}}>> \langle a_1, \dots, a_n \mid r_1, \dots, r_k \rangle @>{\bar\varphi}>> G
\end{CD}
$$
with the composite $F_n \to G$ equal to $\varphi$. The $r_1, \dots, r_k$ form a complete set of relations for the group given by the $a_i$.

## Cayley graphs

:::definition Cayley graph
The **Cayley graph** of $G$ with generators $g_i$: the vertices are the elements of the group, with an edge $s \xrightarrow{\;g_i\;} t$ when $t = s g_i$.
:::

:::example $\ZZ$ with generator $1$
![Cayley graph of Z with generator 1: a line of integers with an arrow from each n to n+1](/notes/math-55a/figures/n3-cayley-z.svg)
:::

:::example $S_3$ with $s_1 = (12)$, $s_2 = (23)$
Edges are multiplication by a generator or its inverse; since $s_1, s_2$ are their own inverses, each edge is an arrow in both directions, drawn as a plain edge.

![Cayley graph of S3 with generators s1 = (12) and s2 = (23): a hexagon e, s1, s1 s2, s1 s2 s1, s2 s1, s2 with edges alternately labelled s1 and s2](/notes/math-55a/figures/n3-cayley-s3.svg)

Here $s_1 = (12)$, $s_2 = (23)$, $s_1 s_2 = (123)$ and $s_2 s_1 = (132)$.
:::
