:::remark
$R$ is a module over itself (free of rank 1).
:::

:::definition
A submodule of $R$ is called an **ideal**. That is, an ideal is a subset $N\subset R$ such that

- $N$ is an abelian subgroup of $(R,+)$;
- $RN\subseteq N$ (multiplication by any $x\in R$ takes $N$ to $N$).
:::

:::example
The ideals in $\ZZ$ are the $n\ZZ$ (and in $k[x]$ they are the $p(x)k[x]$), i.e., each is generated by a single element. This is very special: $\ZZ$ and $k[x]$ are “principal ideal domains”.
:::

## Classification of finitely generated abelian groups

:::note
Think of these as modules, i.e., $\ZZ$-modules, and use the methods of linear algebra over $\ZZ$, since a $\ZZ$-module is the same thing as an abelian group.
:::

:::theorem
Any finitely generated abelian group is isomorphic to a product of cyclic groups:
$$G\cong\big(\ZZ/n_1\times\cdots\times\ZZ/n_k\big)\times\ZZ^{\ell}.$$
:::

$\ZZ/mn\cong\ZZ/m\times\ZZ/n$ if $\gcd(m,n)=1$ (i.e., they are coprime)—this is rather useful.

:::proposition Prop 1
If $M$ is a finitely generated $\ZZ$-module, then there exist $m,n$ and $T\in\Hom(\ZZ^m,\ZZ^n)$ with $M\cong\ZZ^n/\operatorname{Im}(T)$, i.e., there is an exact sequence
$$\ZZ^m\xrightarrow{\ T\ }\ZZ^n\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0.$$
:::
