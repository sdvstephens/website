:::remark Recall
An action of $G$ on $S$ is a map
$$G\times S\to S,\qquad (g,s)\mapsto gs.$$
For $s\in S$, the **orbit** of $s$ is $\mcO_s=\{gs\mid g\in G\}$, and the **stabilizer** is $\operatorname{stab}(s)=\{g\in G\mid gs=s\}$. Then
$$G/\operatorname{stab}(s)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \mcO_s,\qquad [a\operatorname{stab}(s)]\longmapsto as.$$
:::

## Finite subgroups of $SO(3)$ = {rotations of $\RR^3$}

Given $(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$, i.e., a real vector space with a dot product, we get the **orthogonal group**
$$O(V)=\{T\in GL(V)\mid \langle Tu,Tv\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle\ \forall u,v\in V\},\qquad GL(V)=\Aut(V).$$
Here $T^*=T^{-1}$:
$$\langle Tu,Tv\rangle=\langle u,T^*Tv\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle.$$
$T$ preserves lengths and angles. Inside, $O(V)\supset SO(V)$ = orientation-preserving isometries ($\det=1$).

In dimension 2: $O(2)=O(\RR^2)=\{\text{reflections},\ \text{rotations}\}$, and
$$SO(2)=\{\text{rotations}\}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\right\}.$$

Given $T\in O(V)$, there is a decomposition $V=\bigoplus_iV_i$ with $V_i\perp V_j$, $\dim V_i=1$ or $2$, and $T(V_i)=V_i$ (each subspace is invariant). So there is an orthonormal basis in which $T$ is block diagonal with $1\times1$ and $2\times2$ blocks.

$\dim V=3$ $\implies$ the matrix of $T$ is
$$\begin{pmatrix}\pm1&&\\&\pm1&\\&&\pm1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{or}\quad\begin{pmatrix}\pm1&\\&A\end{pmatrix}\ (A\text{ a }2\times2\text{ block}).$$
If $\det=1$: $SO(3)$ = linear operators which, in some orthonormal basis, have matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&\\&A\end{pmatrix}$ with $A\in SO(2)$ (e.g., $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)$). All are rotations by some angle about an axis.

Given a subset $\Sigma\subset\RR^3$, its **symmetry group** is
$$\{T\in SO(3)\mid T(\Sigma)=\Sigma\}.$$

It could be infinite: if $\Sigma$ is a circle in the $xy$-plane, its symmetries are all rotations about the $z$-axis, and the rotations by $180^\circ$ about lines in the $xy$-plane ($\cong O(2)$).

![A circle Sigma in the xy-plane with the z-axis through its centre; it is symmetric under all rotations about z and under half-turns about lines in the plane](/notes/math-55a/figures/n1-circle-symmetry.svg)

But finite is more interesting.

:::example
$\Sigma$ = a regular $n$-gon in the $xy$-plane $\implies$ the symmetry group is $D_n$ (the dihedral group): $n$ rotations about the $z$-axis and $n$ flips ($180^\circ$ rotations about axes in the $xy$-plane). (The degenerate cases $n=2$, a two-sided “lens”, and $n=1$ are drawn too.)

![A regular pentagon in the xy-plane, with the rotation by 2π/n about the z-axis and a flip axis lying in the plane](/notes/math-55a/figures/n1-dihedral.svg)
:::

:::example
$\ZZ/n\subset D_n$: the cyclic group generated by the rotation by $\frac{2\pi}{n}$ (e.g., the symmetries of a pyramid over a regular $n$-gon).

![A pyramid over a regular pentagon with its vertical axis; its rotation group is cyclic, generated by the rotation by 2π/n](/notes/math-55a/figures/n1-pyramid.svg)
:::

## Symmetries of regular polygons; regular polyhedra

![A tetrahedron, a cube and an octahedron](/notes/math-55a/figures/n1-polyhedra.svg)

Duals: for $P\subseteq V$,
$$P^*=\{\ell\in V^*\mid \ell(v)\le1\ \forall v\in P\}\cong\{v\in V\mid\langle v,u\rangle\le1\ \forall u\in P\}.$$

Continuing: the dodecahedron (12 faces; drawn as a ball, but it’s not a circle!) and the icosahedron (20 faces), its dual.

Symmetries of the tetrahedron are given by $A_4\trianglelefteq S_4$; cube: $S_4$; octahedron: also $S_4$; dodecahedron: $A_5$; icosahedron: $A_5$.

:::theorem
This is the complete list of finite subgroups of $SO(3)$:
$$\ZZ/n,\quad D_n,\quad\text{tetrahedron }(A_4),\quad\text{cube }(S_4),\quad\text{icosahedron }(A_5).$$
:::

The **poles** of $T$: the two points where the axis of the rotation $T$ meets the unit sphere (same as in complex??).

![A sphere with a rotation axis through it; the two points where the axis meets the sphere are the poles](/notes/math-55a/figures/n1-poles.svg)

:::lemma
If $v$ is a pole of $g\in G\subset SO(3)$ and $h\in G$, then $h(v)$ is a pole of $hgh^{-1}$.
:::
