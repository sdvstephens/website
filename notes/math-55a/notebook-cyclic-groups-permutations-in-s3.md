$\sigma=(1\,2\,3)$ means $\sigma(1)=2$, $\sigma(2)=3$, $\sigma(3)=1$:
$$(1\,2\,3)=\begin{cases}1\mapsto2\\2\mapsto3\\3\mapsto1\end{cases}$$

If we have $\tau=(1\,2)$, then $\tau(1)=2$, $\tau(2)=1$, $\tau(3)=3$.

:::example
Given $\tau=(1\,2)$ and $\sigma=(1\,2\,3)$ in $S_3$:
$$\begin{aligned}\tau\sigma(1)&=\tau(2)=1,\\ \sigma\tau(1)&=\sigma(2)=3.\end{aligned}$$
Since $3\neq1$, $S_3$ is non-abelian.
:::
