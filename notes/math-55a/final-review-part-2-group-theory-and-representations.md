- (Lec. 1; Artin ch. 2) A **group** $(G,\cdot)$ is a set with an operation $\cdot: G\times G\to G$ such that
  1. there exists $e\in G$, the **identity**, such that $eg=ge=g$ for all $g\in G$;
  2. for all $g\in G$ there exists an **inverse** $g^{-1}\in G$ such that $gg^{-1}=g^{-1}g=e$;
  3. **associativity**: $(ab)c=a(bc)$ for all $a,b,c\in G$.
- A group is **abelian** if $\cdot$ is commutative ($ab=ba$ for all $a,b\in G$).
- *Examples:* $(\ZZ,+)$, $(\ZZ/n,+)$, $(\CC^*,\times)$, the symmetric group $S_n$; $GL_n(\RR)$, etc.; **products** $G\times H$, $\ZZ^n$, …
- (Lec. 2) Like sets, groups can be finite ($\ZZ/n$, $S_n$, …), countable ($\ZZ$, $\ZZ^n$, $\QQ$, …) or uncountable ($\RR$, …).
- $H\subset G$ is a **subgroup** if $e\in H$; $a\in H\Rightarrow a^{-1}\in H$; $a,b\in H\Rightarrow ab\in H$. $|H|$ divides $|G|$.
  - $H,H'$ subgroups of $G$ $\Rightarrow$ $H\cap H'$ is a subgroup of $G$.
  - All subgroups of $(\ZZ,+)$ are $\ZZ n=\{mn\mid m\in\ZZ\}$ for some $n\ge 0$.
- A **homomorphism** $\varphi:G\to H$ is a map such that $\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)$ for all $a,b\in G$ ($\Rightarrow \varphi(a^{-1})=\varphi(a)^{-1}$).
  - **Isomorphism** = bijective homomorphism; **automorphism** = isomorphism $G\xrightarrow{\sim}G$. $(\Aut(G),\circ)$ is a group.
- The **kernel** of $\varphi:G\to H$: $\ker(\varphi)=\{g\in G\mid \varphi(g)=e_H\}$, a subgroup of $G$. $\varphi$ injective $\Leftrightarrow$ $\ker\varphi=\{e\}$.
  - The **image** of $\varphi$: $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\{\varphi(g)\mid g\in G\}\subset H$, a subgroup of $H$. $\varphi$ surjective $\Leftrightarrow$ $\operatorname{Im}\varphi=H$.
- (Lec. 3) Given $a\in G$, $\varphi:\ZZ\to G$, $k\mapsto a^k$, is a homomorphism with $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\langle a\rangle$, the subgroup generated by $a$.
  - $\ker(\varphi)=\ZZ n$, where $n$ = **order** of $a$ = $\min\{n>0 \text{ such that } a^n=e\}$.
  - Hence the **cyclic** group $\langle a\rangle$ is $\simeq\ZZ/n$ if $a$ has order $n$, and $\simeq\ZZ$ if $a$ has infinite order.
  - ($a_1,\dots,a_k$ **generate** $G$ if every element of $G$ is a product of the $a_i$ and their inverses.)
- A subgroup $H\subset G$ determines an **equivalence relation** (axioms: $a\sim a$; $a\sim b\Leftrightarrow b\sim a$; $a\sim b$ and $b\sim c$ $\Rightarrow a\sim c$): (Lec. 4) $a\sim b$ iff $a^{-1}b\in H$, whose equivalence classes are the (left) **cosets** $aH=\{ah\mid h\in H\}$.
  - The **quotient set**: $G/H=\{\text{cosets } aH\}$. The **index** of $H$: $(G:H)=|G/H|=\dfrac{|G|}{|H|}$ if finite.
- If $G$ is finite: $H\subset G$ subgroup $\Rightarrow$ $|H|$ divides $|G|$; $a\in G\Rightarrow \operatorname{ord}(a)\mid |G|$; $|G|=p$ prime $\Rightarrow G\simeq\ZZ/p$.
- A subgroup $H\subset G$ is **normal** $\Leftrightarrow$ $aH=Ha$ for all $a\in G$ (left cosets = right cosets) $\Leftrightarrow$ $aHa^{-1}=H$ for all $a\in G$ (conjugate subgroups).
- The operation $(aH)\cdot(bH)=abH$ makes $G/H$ a *group* iff $H$ is a *normal subgroup*.
- For every homomorphism $\varphi:G\to H$, $\ker(\varphi)=K$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, and $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\simeq G/K$.
  - (Lec. 5) If $\varphi$ is surjective, we have an **exact sequence** $\{1\}\to K\xrightarrow{\ i\ }G\xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }H\to\{1\}$ ($i$ injective, $\varphi$ surjective, $\operatorname{Im}(i)=\ker(\varphi)$).
  - *Examples:* $\{1\}\to H\hookrightarrow G\to G/H\to\{1\}$ ($H$ normal); $0\to\ZZ/m\to\ZZ/mn\to\ZZ/n\to 0$ ($\ZZ/mn\simeq\ZZ/m\times\ZZ/n$ iff $\gcd(m,n)=1$); $\{e\}\to\ZZ/3\to S_3\xrightarrow{\text{sign}}\ZZ/2\to\{e\}$.
  - A homomorphism $G\xrightarrow{\varphi}H$ factors through $G\to G/K\xrightarrow{\bar\varphi}H$ iff $K\subset\ker\varphi$.
- $G$ is **simple** if its only normal subgroups are $\{e\}$ and itself. *Examples:* $\ZZ/p$, $p$ prime; $A_n$, $n\ge 5$.
- *Example:* the **center** $Z(G)=\{z\in G\mid zg=gz\ \forall g\in G\}$ is a normal subgroup (abelian: $zz'=z'z$).
- *Example:* the **commutator subgroup** $[G,G]=\{\prod_{\text{finite}}[a_i,b_i]\}$, where $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, is normal, and $G/[G,G]=\operatorname{Ab}(G)$ (**abelianization**) is the largest abelian quotient of $G$: every $G\xrightarrow{\varphi}H$ with $H$ abelian factors as $G\to\operatorname{Ab}(G)\xrightarrow{\bar\varphi}H$.
- (Lec. 21; Artin 14.7) Every finitely generated abelian group is $\simeq\ZZ^r\times\ZZ/n_1\times\dots\times\ZZ/n_k$ for some $r,n_1,\dots,n_k$.
- (Artin 6.7–6.12) **Group actions**: a $G$-action on a set $S$ is $G\times S\to S$, $(g,s)\mapsto g\cdot s$, such that $e\cdot s=s$ for all $s\in S$ and $(gh)\cdot s=g\cdot(h\cdot s)$ ($\Leftrightarrow$ a homomorphism $\rho:G\to\operatorname{Perm}(S)$).
  - **Faithful** if $\rho$ is injective; **transitive** if for all $s,t\in S$ there exists $g$ such that $gs=t$ (i.e. one orbit).
- (Lec. 22) The **orbit** of $s\in S$ is $O_s=G\cdot s=\{g\cdot s\mid g\in G\}$. These form a partition $S=\bigsqcup$ orbits.
  - The **stabilizer** of $s$ is $\operatorname{Stab}(s)=\{g\in G\mid g\cdot s=s\}$, a subgroup of $G$.
  - Elements in the same orbit have conjugate stabilizer subgroups: $\operatorname{Stab}(g\cdot s)=g\operatorname{Stab}(s)g^{-1}\subset G$.
  - Orbit-stabilizer: if $H=\operatorname{Stab}(s)$, then $G/H\simeq O_s$, $gH\mapsto g\cdot s$, is a bijection; in particular $|O_s|\cdot|\operatorname{Stab}s|=|G|$.
- **Burnside’s lemma** ($G$, $S$ finite): let $S^g=\{s\in S\mid gs=s\}$ be the fixed points of $g\in G$; then $\#\text{orbits}=\dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|S^g|$.
- (Artin ch. 7) $G$ acts on itself by left multiplication. This gives $G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Perm}(G)$, hence every finite group $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_n$, $n=|G|$.
- $G$ acts on itself by **conjugation**: $g$ acts by $h\mapsto ghg^{-1}$.
  - (Lec. 24) Orbits = conjugacy classes; $\operatorname{Stab}(h)=\{g\in G\mid gh=hg\}=Z(h)$, the **centralizer** of $h$.
  - Hence $|G|=\sum_{C\subset G\text{ conj. classes}}|C|$, where for each conjugacy class $|C_h|=\dfrac{|G|}{|Z(h)|}$ divides $|G|$ (the **class equation** of $G$).
- For $p$-groups ($|G|=p^k$), the class equation $\Rightarrow$ $|Z(G)|\ge p$ (the number of conjugacy classes of size $1$). Hence:
  - $|G|=p^2$, $p$ prime $\Rightarrow$ $G$ is abelian ($\simeq\ZZ/p\times\ZZ/p$ or $\ZZ/p^2$).
  - 5 isomorphism classes of groups of order 8: $\ZZ/8$, $\ZZ/4\times\ZZ/2$, $(\ZZ/2)^3$, $D_4$, the quaternion group.
- (Lec. 23) $G\subset SO(3)$ finite subgroup $\Rightarrow$ by considering the action of $G$ on its poles (unit vectors along rotation axes), $G\simeq$ one of $\ZZ/n$, $D_n$ (regular $n$-gon), $A_4$ (tetrahedron), $S_4$ (cube), $A_5$ (dodecahedron/icosahedron).
- (Lec. 24) The **symmetric group** $S_n$ is generated by transpositions $(ij)$, in fact by $s_i=(i\ i{+}1)$.
- For all $\sigma\in S_n$ there is a unique decomposition of $\sigma$ as a product of disjoint cycles $(a_1\dots a_k)$.
  - $\sigma,\tau\in S_n$ are in the same conjugacy class iff they have the same cycle lengths.
- The **alternating group** $A_n=\ker(\text{sign}:S_n\to\ZZ/2)=\{\text{products of an even number of transpositions}\}$.
  - (Lec. 25) A conjugacy class in $S_n$ which consists of even permutations is either 1 or 2 conjugacy classes in $A_n$; it splits into 2 iff the centralizer $Z(\sigma)\subset A_n$ ($\Leftrightarrow$ the cycle lengths of $\sigma$ are all odd and distinct).
- $A_n$ is simple for $n\ge 5$ ($A_4$ isn’t: $\{\id,(ij)(kl)\}\simeq\ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2$ is normal in $A_4$ and $S_4$).
- (Lec. 26) **Sylow theorems**: if $|G|=p^em$ with $p\nmid m$, a **Sylow $p$-subgroup** of $G$ is a subgroup of order $p^e$.
  - Theorem 1: for every prime $p$ dividing $|G|$, $G$ contains a Sylow $p$-subgroup. ($\to$ consequence: $G$ contains an element of order $p$.)
  - Theorem 2: all Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$ are conjugates of each other, and every subgroup of order $p^k$ ($k\le e$) is contained in a Sylow subgroup.
  - Theorem 3: the number $s_p$ of Sylow $p$-subgroups satisfies $s_p\equiv 1\bmod p$ and $s_p\mid m=\dfrac{|G|}{p^e}$.
- If $G$ contains subgroups $N,H$ such that $N\cap H=\{e\}$ (e.g. because $\gcd(|N|,|H|)=1$) and $|G|=|N|\cdot|H|$, then for every $g\in G$ there exist unique $n\in N$, $h\in H$ such that $g=nh$.
  - If $N$ and $H$ are *both* normal in $G$ then $G\simeq N\times H$.
  - If $N$ is normal but not $H$, we have a **semidirect product** $N\rtimes_\varphi H$, with $\varphi:H\to\Aut(N)$ given by conjugation inside $G$: $(n,h)\cdot(n',h')=(n\,\varphi(h)(n'),\,hh')$.
- (Lec. 28) Given $H\subset G$ (e.g. a $p$-Sylow), the number of conjugate subgroups $gHg^{-1}\subset G$ (e.g. all $p$-Sylows) equals $|G/N(H)|$, where $N(H)$ is the **normalizer** $\{g\in G\mid gHg^{-1}=H\}$ (the largest subgroup of $G$ such that $H$ is normal inside $N$).
- *Examples:*
  - $|G|=15$ $\Rightarrow$ the Sylow subgroups of order 3 and 5 are normal ($s_3=s_5=1$) $\Rightarrow$ $G\simeq\ZZ/3\times\ZZ/5$.
  - $|G|=21$ $\Rightarrow$ $s_3\in\{1,7\}$, $s_7=1$, so either $G\simeq\ZZ/3\times\ZZ/7$ or the semidirect product $\ZZ/7\rtimes\ZZ/3$.
  - $|G|=12$ $\Rightarrow$ 1 or 3 2-Sylows, 1 or 4 3-Sylows; one of these is normal $\Rightarrow$ 5 isomorphism classes: $\ZZ/4\times\ZZ/3$, $(\ZZ/2)^2\times\ZZ/3$, $A_4$, $D_6$, $\ZZ/3\rtimes\ZZ/4$.
- (Lec. 29) The **free group** $F_n=\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\rangle=\{\text{all reduced words } a_{i_1}^{m_1}\cdots a_{i_k}^{m_k}\}$ (words in the $a_i^{\pm1}$ never simplify except $a_ia_i^{-1}=a_i^{-1}a_i=1$).
- Any group $G$ with $n$ generators $g_1,\dots,g_n$ is a quotient of $F_n$, via $\varphi:F_n\twoheadrightarrow G$, $a_i\mapsto g_i$.
  - $G$ is **finitely presented** if $\ker(\varphi)$ is generated by a finite set $r_1,\dots,r_k$ and their conjugates.
  - Write $G\simeq\langle g_1,\dots,g_n\mid r_1,\dots,r_k\rangle=F_n/\langle\text{normal subgroup generated by conjugates of the } r_j\rangle$.
- The **Cayley graph** of $G$ with generators $g_i$: vertices = elements of $G$; edges: connect $g$ to $g\cdot g_i$ for all $g\in G$, for all $g_i$.
- A **normal form** for elements of $G=\langle g_1,\dots,g_n\mid r_1,\dots,r_k\rangle$ is a set of words in $g_1^{\pm1},\dots,g_n^{\pm1}$ such that every element of $G$ appears exactly once among these.
- (Lec. 30) *Examples:* $S_n\simeq\langle s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}\mid s_i^2=1,\ s_is_j=s_js_i \text{ for } |i-j|\ge 2,\ s_is_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}\rangle$.
  - $SL_2(\ZZ)$ is generated by $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. $PSL_2(\ZZ)=SL_2(\ZZ)/\{\pm I\}=\langle S,T\mid S^2,(ST)^3\rangle$.
- (Lec. 30; Artin ch. 10; Fulton–Harris ch. 1–2) A **representation** of $G$ is a vector space $V$ on which $G$ acts by linear operators, i.e. a homomorphism $\rho:G\to GL(V)$.
- A **subrepresentation** is a subspace $W\subset V$ invariant under $G$: $g(W)=W$ for all $g\in G$.
  - $V$ is **irreducible** if it has no nontrivial subrepresentations.
- $G$ finite, $V$ finite-dimensional over $\CC$: each $g:V\to V$ has finite order, $g^n=\mathrm{Id}$ $\Rightarrow$ diagonalizable, with $\lambda_j=e^{2\pi ik/n}$.
- (Lec. 31) If $G$ is abelian, all operators $g:V\to V$ are simultaneously diagonalizable $\Rightarrow$ irreducible representations are 1-dimensional.
  - These correspond to elements of the **dual group** $\widehat G=\Hom(G,\CC^*)$. (Note $\widehat{\ZZ/m}$ is $\simeq\ZZ/m$.)
- A **homomorphism** of representations is a $G$-**equivariant** linear map, i.e. $\varphi(gv)=g\varphi(v)$.
- $V,W$ representations of $G$ $\Rightarrow$ so are $V\oplus W$, $V\otimes W$ ($g: v\otimes w\mapsto gv\otimes gw$), $V^*$ ($\ell\mapsto\ell\circ g^{-1}$), $V^*\otimes W\simeq\Hom(V,W)$ ($\varphi\mapsto g\circ\varphi\circ g^{-1}$). ($\Hom_G(V,W)$ = the invariant part $\Hom(V,W)^G$.)
- Any $\CC$-representation of a finite group $G$ admits an invariant Hermitian inner product, with respect to which $G$ acts by unitary operators.
- $V$ a representation of a finite group (over $\CC$), $W\subset V$ an invariant subspace $\Rightarrow$ there exists $U\subset V$ invariant such that $V=U\oplus W$.
  - Hence any $\CC$-representation of a finite group decomposes into a direct sum of irreducibles.
- (Lec. 32) **Schur’s lemma**: $V,W$ irreducible representations of $G$ $\Rightarrow$ any homomorphism $\varphi\in\Hom_G(V,W)$ is either zero or an isomorphism; and all isomorphisms of an irreducible representation are multiples of $\id$: $\Hom_G(V,V)=\CC\cdot\id_V$.
- *Example:* representations of $S_n$:
  - the trivial representation $U=\CC$, $\sigma$ acts by $\id$;
  - the alternating representation $U'=\CC$, $\sigma$ acts by $(-1)^\sigma$;
  - the standard representation (dimension $n-1$): $V=\{(z_1,\dots,z_n)\mid\sum z_i=0\}\subset\CC^n$, $\sigma$ acts by permuting coordinates: $e_i\mapsto e_{\sigma(i)}$.
  - $U$, $U'$, $V$ are the only irreducible representations of $S_3$.
- (Lec. 33) The key tool to study representations is the **character** $\chi_V:G\to\CC$, $\chi_V(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g:V\to V)$.
  - (In terms of eigenvalues, $\operatorname{tr}(g)=\sum\lambda_i$ and $\operatorname{tr}(g^k)=\sum\lambda_i^k$, so $\chi_V$ recovers all symmetric polynomial expressions in the $\lambda_i$, hence the $\lambda_i$ as an unordered tuple.)
  - $\chi_V:G\to\CC$ is a **class function**, i.e. $\chi_V(hgh^{-1})=\chi_V(g)$.
- $\chi_{V\oplus W}=\chi_V+\chi_W$, $\chi_{V\otimes W}=\chi_V\chi_W$, $\chi_{V^*}=\overline{\chi_V}$, $\chi_{\Hom(V,W)}=\overline{\chi_V}\chi_W$.
- For a permutation representation ($G$ acting on $S$ $\rightsquigarrow$ $G$ acts on $V$ with basis $(e_s)_{s\in S}$, $g\cdot e_s=e_{g\cdot s}$): $\chi(g)=\#\{s\in S\mid g\cdot s=s\}=|S^g|$.
- (Lec. 34) **Character table** of $G$ = list, for each irreducible representation $V_i$, of the value of $\chi_{V_i}$ on each conjugacy class.
- $\varphi=\dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g:V\to V$ is the projection onto $V^G=\{v\in V\mid gv=v\ \forall g\}$, so $\dim(V^G)=\operatorname{tr}(\varphi)=\dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_g\chi_V(g)$.
- $H(\alpha,\beta)=\dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\alpha(g)}\beta(g)$ is a Hermitian inner product on $\CC_{\text{class}}(G)=\{\text{class functions } G\to\CC\}$.
  - Then $\dim\Hom_G(V,W)=H(\chi_V,\chi_W)$.
- The characters of the irreducible representations of $G$ are an **orthonormal basis** of $(\CC_{\text{class}}(G),H)$.
  - In particular, the number of irreducible representations = the number of conjugacy classes.
- The multiplicities $a_i$ in the decomposition of a $G$-representation $W$ into irreducibles, $W\simeq\bigoplus_i V_i^{\oplus a_i}$, are given by $a_i=\dim\Hom_G(V_i,W)=H(\chi_{V_i},\chi_W)$. Moreover, $H(\chi_W,\chi_W)=\sum a_i^2$.
- The **regular representation** of $G$ (= the permutation representation for $G$ acting on itself by left multiplication) contains each irreducible representation $V_i$ with multiplicity $=\dim V_i$; therefore $|G|=\sum_i(\dim V_i)^2$.
- (Lec. 35) These results allow us to find character tables of various groups (e.g. $S_4$, $A_4$, $S_5$, $A_5$) by starting from known representations, considering tensor products, and using $H(\cdot,\cdot)$ pairings and orthogonality to find irreducible pieces and the missing irreducible representations.
