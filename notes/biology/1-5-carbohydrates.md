## Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, typically in a ratio of $\ce{CH2O}$. They serve two main functions: **energy storage** and **structural support**. The monomers of carbohydrates are **monosaccharides** (simple sugars).

### Monosaccharides

Monosaccharides are single sugar units with the general formula $\ce{(CH2O)_n}$ where $n = 3$ to $7$. The most important monosaccharides in biology are hexoses ($n = 6$):

- **Glucose** ($\ce{C6H12O6}$): The primary fuel for cellular respiration
- **Fructose**: Found in fruits; same formula as glucose but different structure (isomer)
- **Galactose**: Component of lactose; also an isomer of glucose

**Glucose exists in multiple forms.** In aqueous solution, glucose interconverts between an open-chain form and two cyclic forms:

- **Open chain**: The aldehyde form; rarely present in solution
- **$\alpha$-glucose** ($\alpha$-cyclic): The $\ce{-OH}$ on carbon 1 points *down* (axial); comprises $\sim 36\%$ of glucose at equilibrium
- **$\beta$-glucose** ($\beta$-cyclic): The $\ce{-OH}$ on carbon 1 points *up* (equatorial); comprises $\sim 64\%$ of glucose at equilibrium—the dominant form in cells

The $\alpha$ vs $\beta$ distinction matters: starch and glycogen use $\alpha$-linkages (digestible), while cellulose uses $\beta$-linkages (indigestible to most animals).

### Disaccharides

Disaccharides are formed when two monosaccharides join via a **glycosidic linkage** (a condensation reaction releasing $\ce{H2O}$):

- **Maltose** = glucose + glucose (found in germinating seeds)
- **Sucrose** = glucose + fructose (table sugar)
- **Lactose** = glucose + galactose (milk sugar)

### Polysaccharides

Polysaccharides are long chains of monosaccharides. Their function depends on their structure:

**Energy storage:**

- **Starch**: Energy storage in plants; made of glucose units
- **Glycogen**: Energy storage in animals (liver and muscles); highly branched glucose polymer

**Structural:**

- **Cellulose**: Structural component of plant cell walls; glucose units linked differently than in starch, making it indigestible to most animals
- **Chitin**: Structural component of fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons; contains nitrogen
