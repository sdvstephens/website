The structure of this book is a bottom-up approach, i.e., we start with the cells, move on to the tissue, then organs, etc.

The brain is by far the most complex and sophisticated structure in the human body (and perhaps the known universe).

The nervous system consists of two cell types (which may be broken down into smaller subtypes) called **neurons** and **glia**. Neurons are known to be the main cells responsible for the functions of the brain, while, loosely and not entirely accurately, the glia (**glial cells**) are the glue holding the neurons together.

## Histology

**Formaldehyde** is used to harden the jelly-like brain so that it can be observed in thin slices cut with a microtome.

- **Microtome:** a device that uses steel, glass, or diamond knives to cut microscopically thin slices of tissue. Now we also use femtosecond lasers and other techniques.
- **Nissl bodies:** dyed clumps of material surrounding the nuclei of neurons.
- **Nissl stain:** stains/dyes used on neurons and glia to differentiate the cytoarchitecture of the cells/tissue.
- **Cytoarchitecture:** the structure and arrangement of cells.
- **Histology:** the study of microscopic structures in tissue.
- **Golgi stain:** a silver chromate solution used to dye a small percentage of neurons accurately.
- **Neurohistology:** exactly how it sounds.

> “The gain in the brain is mainly in stain.”

## The parts of a neuron

The neuron is made up of a central body and the long tubes which flow from the central body. The central body is called the **cell body**, the **soma** (plural **somata**), and the **perikaryon** (plural **perikarya**) (lots of names for one thing). The tubes are called **neurites** and are divided into two types: **axons** and **dendrites**.

Axons can be very long (up to 1 m or more) and are uniform. There is usually one axon per cell body. Dendrites are rarely longer than 2 mm, taper off into a point, and are large in number.

Axons were believed to be the “wires” that carry the output of the neuron/cell body.

Dendrites were then believed to be receiving input, acting as the antennae.

## The neuron doctrine

:::definition Neuron doctrine
The **neuron doctrine**: cell theory also applies to neurons (contact, not continuity).
:::

Santiago Ramón y Cajal and Golgi argued over this (with Cajal being right). It was not until the 1950s and the advent of the electron microscope that this was resolved.

## Scales and microscopes

In meters:

$$\overset{10^{3}}{\text{km}} \to \overset{1}{\text{m}} \to \overset{10^{-2}}{\text{cm}} \to \overset{10^{-3}}{\text{mm}} \to \overset{10^{-6}}{\mu\text{m}} \to \overset{10^{-9}}{\text{nm}}$$

100 µm is the near-limit of the naked eye.

Neurons are 4–20 µm.

Light microscopes have a limit of 0.1 µm, but the space between neurons is 20 nm (0.02 µm).

The electron microscope uses an electron beam and has a limit of 0.1 nm. The structure of the brain at this size is called the **ultrastructure**.

Laser microscopy can go to 20 nm across and works on non-fixed, living tissue.
