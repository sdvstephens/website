*Lecture 4 notes.*

The primary cells of the nervous system gain their speciality from the morphology of their neurites and the physiology of their electrical excitability. For glia, the specialized morphology is that of oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells, while their specialized physiology is that of astrocytes: calcium waves.

## Astrocytes

**Astrocytes** come in two types:

1. **Protoplasmic:** grey matter.
2. **Fibrous:** white matter.

Main functions:

- control local blood flow (**neurovascular coupling**);
- metabolic support (**neurometabolic coupling**): the lactate shuttle, glycogen;
- homeostasis (potassium buffering);
- modulate synaptic activity (the **tripartite synapse**);
- respond to injury.

They are critical for normal function. Example: **Alexander disease**, a rare leukodystrophy, is a genetic disease affecting the growth/development of myelin (mutations).

## Microglia

**Microglia** serve the function of macrophages in the CNS. They make up 5–25% of brain cells in the mouse (they derive from myeloid progenitors of the embryonic yolk sac). They become reactive after CNS injury and activate nearby astrocytes; after injury, astrocytes develop “scars.” They be patrolling around.

**Markers:** GFAP for astrocytes; OX-18 for microglia.
