*STEMS that be celling.*

Stem cells are in the epidermis, hair, and **melanocytes** (melanin). **Fibroblasts**, yay! **Arrector pili** muscles: goosebumps. Systemic changes are of interest; skin allows us to see aging-based systemic changes easily.

:::question
Can we regenerate multilineage organs in injured animals?
:::

Levels of regeneration:

- whole body (ex: that one worm);
- organ level (ex: axolotl): the goal;
- tissue level (ex: some skin).

Most cell types do not regenerate after wounding. Other than the epidermis, cells do not regenerate normally (ex: fibroblasts are never the same after damage). How to tell weaker (wounded) vs. stronger skin: stretch it until it breaks.

Wound ⇒ weaker collagen ⇒ more wrinkles.

Wounds at embryonic stages are able to (almost, virtually entirely) fully heal:

| Stage | Healing |
|---|---|
| E16.5 (embryonic) | full heal |
| P5 (1 week post-natal) | no heal |

**Post-natal wound fibroblasts (PWFs)** are wound-specific post-natal populations: they don’t appear in embryonic wounds or unwounded skin.

- Gene analysis of this via overexpression (of genes from vectors in embryonic wounds): K14 AAV-tdTomato.
- 3 genes inhibited it in embryonic wounds: *Ccl7*, *Cxcl12*, *Timp1*. Their overexpression inhibits regeneration, and it happens automatically post-natally. It’s not for immune cells (except *Timp1*, only a little bit), so why?

These may be important for the immune system, but the effect is not mediated in or by the immune system.

The 3 genes all lead to hyperinnervation inside wounds. AAV-*Ngf* (kinda a 4th gene): hyperinnervation blocks regeneration.

What causes this gene expression over time? We don’t know yet.

So we have made regeneration worse; let’s try to make it better.

- *Cxcl12* knockout: the immune system stays the same, and hyperinnervation is significantly reduced. Results: NEAR COMPLETE post-natal multilineage regeneration (not as good as embryonic yet).
- Doing the same with *Cxcr4* from neurons does the same for wounds!
- So: embryonic wounds regenerate at the organ level.
