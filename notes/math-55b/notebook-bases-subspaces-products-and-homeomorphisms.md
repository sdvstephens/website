## Basis

:::definition Basis
A **basis** is a collection of opens $\mcB\subset\mcT$ such that $\mcB$ satisfies

1. for all $x\in X$ there exists $B\in\mcB$ such that $x\in B$;
2. if $B_1,B_2\in\mcB$ and $x\in B_1\cap B_2$, then there exists $B'\in\mcB$ such that $x\in B'\subset B_1\cap B_2$.
:::

$$\mcT=\{\text{unions of basis opens}\}=\{U\subset X\mid \forall x\in U\ \exists B\in\mcB \text{ s.t. } x\in B\subset U\}.$$

## Subspace topology

:::definition Subspace topology
For $Y\subset X$, define
$$\mcT_Y=\{U\cap Y\mid U\in\mcT_X\}.$$
:::

## Product topology

:::definition Product topology
The **product topology** on $X\times Y$ (each with $\mcT_X,\mcT_Y$) is generated by the basis
$$\mcB=\{U\times V\mid U\subset X,\ V\subset Y\ \text{open}\}.$$
:::

![Two overlapping open rectangles, and a disk covered by infinitely many rectangles](/notes/math-55b/figures/b1-product-basis.svg)

It takes infinitely many rectangles to cover a ball. The intersection of two basis elements is again one:
$$(U_1\times V_1)\cap(U_2\times V_2)=(U_1\cap U_2)\times(V_1\cap V_2).$$

If $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ are metric spaces, we can build
$$d^\infty_{X\times Y}\big((x,y),(x',y')\big)=\max\big(d_X(x,x'),\,d_Y(y,y')\big),$$
a distance whose metric topology is the product topology. Balls in $X\times Y$ = (balls in $X$) $\times$ (balls in $Y$).

We could also use
$$d^2_{X\times Y}=\sqrt{d_X^2+d_Y^2}\qquad\text{or}\qquad d^1_{X\times Y}=d_X+d_Y.$$
These also define the product topology. Similarly for $X_1\times\cdots\times X_n$, a finite product.

## Homeomorphisms

:::definition Homeomorphism
A **homeomorphism** is an isomorphism of topological spaces: $X$ and $Y$ are **homeomorphic** if there exist continuous bijections $f\colon X\to Y$, $g\colon Y\to X$ with $g\circ f=\id_X$, $f\circ g=\id_Y$.
:::

Under a homeomorphism, open sets correspond to open sets: for $U\subset X$ and $V\subset Y$ matched up by $f$ and $g$,
$$\begin{aligned}
U\subset X\quad&\longleftrightarrow\quad V\subset Y\\
U\text{ open}\quad&\Longleftarrow\quad V\text{ open}\qquad(f\text{ continuous})\\
U\text{ open}\quad&\Longrightarrow\quad V\text{ open}\qquad(g\text{ continuous})
\end{aligned}$$

:::remark
The inverse of a continuous bijection needn’t be continuous. Take $X$ with topologies $\mcT\subsetneq\mcT'$ ($\mcT'$ strictly finer). Then $\id_X\colon(X,\mcT')\to(X,\mcT)$ is continuous, but not a homeomorphism.
:::

## Boundedness is not topological

:::definition Bounded
A metric space $(X,d)$ is **bounded** if the **diameter**
$$\diam(X)=\sup\{d(p,q)\mid p,q\in X\}<\infty.$$
:::

This is **not** a topological property, and thus will not be invariant under homeomorphism.

:::example
$f=\tan\colon\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\to\RR$ is a homeomorphism.
:::
